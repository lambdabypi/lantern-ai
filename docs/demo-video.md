# Demo video: script and shot list

About 2:45. Recorded on the live demo with a fresh registered account and the example household (made-up people,
real recall data). Voiceover is Shreyas.

## Before recording

- Fresh account, then **See a demo household**. Turn off other notifications. Desktop at 1440×900, or a phone.
- Have the test label photo ready to upload (the crib mattress label: "Product Model: BYL001").
- **Rules check:** no third-party logos, and don't show other websites. That means not opening nhtsa.gov,
  Google Maps or a maker's site on camera. Stay inside Lantern; the links can be shown, just not opened.
- Car and product names appear in the app as data (make, model, brand). Logos never do. If you want to be extra
  safe, keep those shots short.

## Script

| Time | On screen | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Landing page, the light rising | About one in five cars on U.S. roads has a safety recall that nobody fixed. The repair is free. Most people just never hear about it. |
| 0:10 | The "What do you own?" box | Thing is, recall notices go out by mail or press release, and only about 6% of products recalled that way get fixed. Tell owners directly and it's closer to 50%. So I built the direct notice. |
| 0:22 | Tap the mic, say "We have a 2018 Honda Civic, a crib and a space heater", the words fill the box, press the arrow | This is Lantern. You tell it what you own, by typing, talking, or taking a photo of a label. |
| 0:35 | Home with the example household, the red "Do not drive" card first | Every day it checks the official U.S. recall lists for everything you own. The worst one goes first. This truck has a "Do not drive" recall, and NVIDIA Nemotron explains it in plain words, with the official notice one tap away. |
| 0:52 | Open a recall row, then "Is mine affected?" on the crib mattress | And here's the part recall apps skip. A recall covers a model. You own one unit. So I ask, is mine affected? |
| 1:02 | Upload the label photo, the verdict "Yours is included" with the quote | I take a photo of the label. Nemotron Super VL reads it, Nemotron Super checks it against the notice, and it quotes the exact line that decides it. It only ever says "not included" if it can quote the notice word for word. |
| 1:20 | The "What other owners report" card for the Tucson | Recalls also come late. Owners complain to NHTSA first. Lantern reads those complaints for your model and flags the problems no recall covers yet. Two hundred engine complaints, eight fires, no recall. |
| 1:37 | "Show me how to get the fix", the numbered steps with sources | Then it helps you actually fix it. Tavily reads the maker's own recall page, and Nemotron turns it into steps, citing where each one came from. |
| 1:50 | "Get it fixed for free": dealers near a ZIP and the drafted message | For a car, it finds authorized dealers near you and writes the booking message with your VIN and every recall number. You send it yourself. Lantern never sends anything for you. |
| 2:02 | Ask tab: "Is my mom's car safe to drive?", the streamed answer | You can ask about anything on your list. This is Mom's car, so the answer is about her car, and it says what to tell her. |
| 2:15 | Menu, Skills: the switches, then one SKILL.md | Every feature is a skill you can see and switch off. Off really means off. |
| 2:22 | My stuff, "Advanced: edit the file", the Markdown file | And everything it remembers is a plain file you can read, edit, delete or download. No black box. |
| 2:30 | Plain card or README section: Nano, Super, Super VL, Ultra on Token Factory; Tavily | Under the hood, every model call runs on Nemotron through Nebius Token Factory. Nano for the quick reads, Super for writing answers, Super VL for photos, Ultra when you ask it to think harder. Tavily reads the makers' pages. |
| 2:42 | Back to Home, the "Watching every day" note | Recall apps tell you a recall exists. Lantern tells you what it means for you, and helps you get it fixed. |
| 2:52 | Name and link | Lantern. Open source, and free to try. |

## Notes

- If anything is slow live, cut the wait: the first check of a new car takes about 7 seconds.
- The numbers in the voiceover are from the README's sources (CARFAX for 1 in 5; CPSC figures for 6% vs 50%) and
  from the live demo (Tucson engine complaints). Re-read the Tucson card on the day, since the counts update weekly.
