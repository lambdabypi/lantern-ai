About safety recalls

Short answers, with links to the official sources.

What is a safety recall?

When a car, a car part or a household product turns out to have a safety defect, or doesn't meet U.S. safety standards, the maker announces a recall and has to offer owners a fix. For cars this is overseen by NHTSA, the U.S. road safety agency. For most household products it's the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC).

Sources: NHTSA recalls · CPSC recalls

Is the fix really free?

Cars: for most cars, yes. The maker pays for the recall repair at any authorized dealer for your brand, whether you bought the car new or used, and not just the one that sold it.

By law the free repair may not apply if the car was first sold more than 15 years before the recall. Makers often fix older cars anyway, so ask the dealer.

Products: the company offers a remedy in the recall notice, usually a refund, a repair or a replacement.

Sources: Kelley Blue Book recall FAQ · 49 U.S.C. § 30120 · CPSC recalls

What does "Do not drive" mean?

The maker and NHTSA say the defect is dangerous enough that the car shouldn't be driven at all until it's repaired. Call a dealer for your brand and ask about a tow or a mobile repair.

Not sure if yours was already fixed? The official VIN check shows it, and so can any dealer for your brand.

Sources: NHTSA recalls · CarPro on NHTSA's urgent recall alerts

What does "Park outside" mean?

There's a risk of fire, even when the car is switched off. Park it outside, away from garages and buildings, until it's repaired.

Source: CarPro on NHTSA's urgent recall alerts

What does "Stop using" mean?

The official recall notice tells owners to stop using the product right away. The notice also says how to get the remedy, and Lantern shows it under "What to do".

Source: the CPSC notice for each recall (linked on every recall).

What's a VIN, and where do I find it?

It's your car's ID number: 17 letters and numbers. You'll find it on your registration or insurance card, on the dashboard where it meets the windshield (driver's side, seen from outside), and on the driver's door frame.

Source: NAPA: where is my VIN?

Why can't Lantern tell if my car was already fixed?

The government's recall list is by make, model and year. Whether your particular car has been repaired is only shown by the official VIN check, or by any dealer for your brand. If it was fixed, tap "A dealer fixed this" and Lantern stops warning you.

I bought it used. Does the recall still apply?

Yes. A recall stays with the car. If the last owner never had it fixed, you can get the repair, and for most cars it's free.

Source: Kelley Blue Book recall FAQ

What about child car seats and tires?

Lantern doesn't check them yet. Check them at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

Who writes the plain-English summaries?

NVIDIA Nemotron, an open AI model running on Nebius Token Factory, rewrites each official notice in everyday words, using only what the notice says. The official notice is always one tap away, under the summary.

What happens to my data?

Your list is kept as plain files, and you can download or delete it any time. To check recalls, Lantern sends only a make, model and year (or a VIN) to NHTSA. Chat messages go to Nebius Token Factory to write replies, and dealer searches go to Tavily. Lantern never contacts anyone for you.
